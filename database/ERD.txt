===============================================================================
 ADVANCE POS SYSTEM (AdvPOS) -- ENTITY RELATIONSHIP DIAGRAM (text)
 84 tables. PostgreSQL. No enums, no triggers, no procedures.
===============================================================================

HOW TO READ THIS
  A ──< B      one A has many B          (B holds the foreign key)
  A ──- B      one A has exactly one B   (B's primary key IS the foreign key)
  A >──< B     many-to-many, resolved by the junction table named between them
  [PK]         primary key
  [FK]         foreign key
  [U]          unique constraint
  (CASCADE)    deleting the parent deletes these children
  (SET NULL)   deleting the parent leaves the child, with the link emptied

 1. THE SPINE:  Role  ->  User  ->  Employee / Party
===============================================================================

This is the shape you asked for. One role table, one user table beneath it,
and then two subtype tables because a member of staff and a shop are not the
same kind of thing even though both are users.

    Role  [PK role_id]                       7 rows
      |   role_key, role_name, description, home_path,
      |   is_staff_role, requires_email, is_system
      |   [U] role_key
      |   [U] (role_id, requires_email)  <-- target of the rule below
      |
      +──< User  [PK user_id]                32 rows
      |     role_id [FK]  (CASCADE)
      |     requires_email  -- mirrors Role, held in place by a composite FK
      |     full_name, email [U], phone, password_hash,
      |     primary_location_id [FK -> Location] (SET NULL),
      |     is_active, created_at
      |          |
      |          +──- Employee [PK/FK user_id]      11 rows   (CASCADE)
      |          |      employee_code [U], is_locked, joined_on, last_login_at
      |          |
      |          +──- Party    [PK/FK user_id]      21 rows   (CASCADE)
      |                 party_code [U], legal_name, display_name,
      |                 category_id [FK -> PartyCategory],
      |                 city_id [FK -> City],
      |                 ntn [U], strn [U], cnic [U],
      |                 credit_limit, credit_days,
      |                 hold_policy_id [FK -> CreditHoldPolicy],
      |                 opening_balance, rating,
      |                 sales_person_user_id [FK -> Employee] (SET NULL),
      |                 default_location_id  [FK -> Location] (SET NULL)
      |
      +──< RolePermission >──── Permission [PK permission_id]   39 rows
      |      [PK (role_id, permission_id)]                      93 links
      |
      +──< DeliveryChannel.confirmed_by_role_id


  THE E-MAIL RULE (your example, enforced without a single trigger)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Super Admin, Accountant, Order Department and Sales sign in, so their
  address is mandatory and unique. Customers and Suppliers are opened by the
  Super Admin off a phone call and very often have no e-mail at all.

  Three constraints do it between them:

    1.  Role has  [U] (role_id, requires_email)
    2.  User has  FK (role_id, requires_email) -> Role (role_id, requires_email)
                  ON UPDATE CASCADE
    3.  User has  CHECK (requires_email = FALSE OR email IS NOT NULL)

  A Sales user with a NULL e-mail fails the CHECK. A staff row that tries to
  claim the customer rule by setting requires_email = FALSE fails the foreign
  key, because no such pair exists in Role. Flip the flag on Role and the
  CASCADE rewrites every user of that role at once, so the two can never drift.
  UNIQUE on email still forbids two people sharing one address, and PostgreSQL
  allows any number of NULLs in a unique column, so every party may be blank.

  Verified against PostgreSQL 18 -- all four cases behave as described.


  Other links off a user:
    User ──< UserLocation >──── Location    (a user may work several places)
    User ──< UserPreference                 (notification switches)
    User ──< Notification
    User ──< ActivityLog                    (SET NULL: the system posts too)

 5. ACCOUNTING -- ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH
===============================================================================

    AccountGroup [PK group_id]  5      (Assets, Capital, Expenses,
       |                                 Liabilities, Revenue)
       └──< AccountType [PK account_type_id]  14
              |  type_name [U], code_prefix [U], code_length,
              |  is_debit_normal, is_system
              |
              └──< Account [PK account_id]  52
                     account_code [U], account_name,
                     parent_account_id [FK -> Account]   (self-referencing tree)
                     is_group, opening_balance, currency_code

    Account carries an opening_balance AND NOTHING ELSE. There is no stored
    running balance, because a stored balance is a second truth that drifts
    the first time somebody edits an entry.

    JournalEntryType [PK]  7    PostingStatus [PK]  6    FiscalPeriod [PK]  8
            \                        |                        /
             \                       |                       /
              +─────────────> JournalEntry [PK entry_id]  35
                                |  entry_no [U], entry_date, entry_type_id [FK],
                                |  period_id [FK], location_id [FK],
                                |  reference_no, narration, status_id [FK],
                                |  created_by_user_id [FK], posted_by_user_id [FK]
                                |
                                └──< JournalEntryLine [PK line_id]  106
                                       [U] (entry_id, line_no)
                                       account_id [FK -> Account]
                                       party_user_id [FK -> Party] (SET NULL)
                                       debit_amount, credit_amount
                                       CHECK: a line is a debit OR a credit,
                                              never both and never neither

    EVERYTHING FINANCIAL IS A QUERY OVER THOSE TWO TABLES
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
      Ledger         WHERE account_id = ?           ORDER BY entry_date
      Trial balance  SUM(debit), SUM(credit)        GROUP BY account_id
      Manual entry   a JournalEntry of type JOURNAL, keyed on the screen
      Statement      WHERE party_user_id = ?
      P&L / Balance  the same sums rolled up  Account -> AccountType -> AccountGroup

    Verified on the seed: 35 entries, 106 lines, every entry balances, and the
    trial balance comes out 5,323,575.00 debit against 5,323,575.00 credit.

    The documents that MOVE money all hang off the same ledger:

    VoucherType [PK]  7
       └──< Voucher [PK voucher_id]  15
              |  voucher_no [U], voucher_date, location_id [FK],
              |  party_user_id [FK -> Party] (SET NULL),
              |  cash_bank_account_id [FK -> Account]  -- NULL only on a JV,
              |                                           which touches no drawer
              |  amount, method_id [FK], status_id [FK],
              |  entry_id [FK -> JournalEntry] (SET NULL)
              |
              └──< VoucherAllocation [PK allocation_id]  13
                     sales_invoice_id    [FK -> SalesInvoice]
                     purchase_invoice_id [FK -> PurchaseInvoice]
                     amount
                     CHECK: exactly one of the two, never both

    THIS is what "paid" means. No invoice stores a paid or balance column:

        SELECT SUM(amount) FROM "VoucherAllocation" WHERE sales_invoice_id = ?

    Expense [PK expense_id]  6
       expense_account_id [FK -> Account], paid_from_account_id [FK -> Account],
       method_id [FK], status_id [FK], entry_id [FK] (SET NULL)
       CHECK: the two accounts must differ

    Collection [PK collection_id]  8      -- money a rep took in the field
       |  receipt_no [U], customer_user_id [FK -> Party],
       |  collected_by_user_id [FK -> Employee], amount, method_id [FK],
       |  cheque_date, status_id [FK -> CollectionStatus],
       |  confirmed_on, confirmed_by_user_id [FK] (SET NULL),
       |  voucher_id [FK -> Voucher] (SET NULL)
       |
       └──< CollectionAllocation [PK allocation_id]  7
              order_id [FK -> SalesOrder], amount

       The rep's entry does NOT move the customer's ledger. It waits for
       Accounts to confirm, and only then becomes a Voucher and an entry.
       voucher_id is NULL until that happens. Without that gap a rep could sit
       on cash for a week and the books would still look settled.

 8. DELIVERY, CLAIMS, FIELD WORK
===============================================================================

    DeliveryStatus [PK]  8   NOT_DISPATCHED, BOOKED, AWAITING, IN_TRANSIT,
       |                     OUT_FOR_DELIVERY, DELIVERED, FAILED,
       |                     RETURNED_TO_SENDER
       └──< Delivery [PK delivery_id]  11   delivery_no [U]
              order_id [FK -> SalesOrder] (CASCADE)
              invoice_id [FK -> SalesInvoice] (SET NULL)
              channel_id [FK], courier_id [FK] (SET NULL),
              tracking_no, booked_date, expected_date, delivered_date,
              parcels, weight_kg, cod_amount, is_cod_settled,
              booking_charge, reminders_sent,
              confirmed_by_user_id [FK -> Employee] (SET NULL)

       Everything about how the goods left and whether they arrived lives on
       ONE row. SalesOrder never repeats a carrier or a tracking number. No
       Delivery row means the order has not been dispatched.

       AWAITING and IN_TRANSIT are the two states the business added on top of
       the courier's own: "we sent it and nobody has confirmed" against
       "somebody checked, it is still moving". The gap between those two is
       precisely what this system was built to see.

    ClaimStage [PK] 6   ClaimReason [PK] 7   ClaimOutcome [PK] 3
          \                    |                   /
           Claim [PK claim_id]  12    claim_no [U]
             customer_user_id [FK -> Party], received_by_user_id [FK -> Employee],
             product_id [FK], quantity, unit_cost,
             original_order_no  -- FREE TEXT, and almost always NULL
             supplier_user_id [FK -> Party] (SET NULL),
             sent_on, settled_on, supplier_note, reminders_sent
             CHECK: settled_on requires sent_on; sent_on requires a supplier

       A claim is recorded against the ITEM, never against an order. A
       shopkeeper brings back one dead battery months later and has no idea
       which invoice it came on, and does not care. That is why there is no
       foreign key to an order here -- only an optional scrap of text.

 10. DELETE BEHAVIOUR -- nothing ever blocks
===============================================================================

    Every foreign key is ON UPDATE CASCADE. On delete there are two rules:

    ON DELETE CASCADE  where the child is owned by the parent and is
                       meaningless without it:
                         SalesOrder      -> SalesOrderItem, Delivery,
                                            CollectionAllocation, SalesInvoice
                         SalesInvoice    -> SalesInvoiceItem, SalesReturn,
                                            VoucherAllocation
                         JournalEntry    -> JournalEntryLine
                         User            -> Employee, Party, and everything
                                            hanging off them
                         Product         -> ProductBarcode, StockBalance
                         Category/Account-> their own children (self-reference)

    ON DELETE SET NULL where the reference is optional and the child stands
                       on its own without it:
                         Location -> User.primary_location_id
                         Location -> ActivityLog.location_id
                         Employee -> Party.sales_person_user_id
                         Employee -> Delivery.confirmed_by_user_id
                         JournalEntry -> Voucher.entry_id, Expense.entry_id
                         PurchaseOrder -> GoodsReceipt.po_id

    Verified: deleting order 1 removed its 3 items, its delivery, its
    collection allocation and its invoice, and nothing raised an error.
    Deleting customer 12 removed the party row, their orders, their claims and
    their collections. Deleting a location left all 32 users standing with the
    link emptied.

    A WORD OF WARNING, because it follows from what you asked for:
    with CASCADE everywhere, DELETE on master data is destructive by design.
    Deactivate instead of deleting -- Product, Party, Location, Courier,
    Category, Brand and Account all carry is_active for exactly that reason.


===============================================================================
 11. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT HERE
===============================================================================

    STATUS HISTORY.  Every document carries its CURRENT status as a foreign
    key to a status table -- SalesOrder.status_id, Claim.stage_id,
    Delivery.status_id and so on. What it does not carry is the trail of how
    it got there: who marked ORD-26-0142 dispatched, and when.

    That is answered separately, in 04_status_history.sql, which is not part
    of the schema above and does not need to be loaded.

    Nothing else from the frontend is missing. Reminders are derived rather
    than stored, for the reason given in section 9.
