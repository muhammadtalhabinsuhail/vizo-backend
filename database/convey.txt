 1.  THE THREE FAULTS BEHIND WHAT YOU REPORTED, AND WHAT EACH ONE ACTUALLY WAS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 You reported four symptoms. They came from three faults, and none of the three
 was where the symptom pointed. Worth writing down, because two of them had
 been there since the order workflow shipped on 3 September.


 1.1  "CLICKING INVOICED DOES NOT PRODUCE AN INVOICE"

      It never did. PATCH /sales/orders/{id}/status wrote a status id and
      nothing else. There is a separate POST /orders/{id}/invoice that does the
      real work — cuts the invoice, numbers it, renders the bill, pushes it to
      Cloudinary — and the chain strip on the order screen never called it.

      So pressing step 4 of the chain, which is the obvious way to bill an
      order and the one thing on screen, left an order that SAID "Invoiced"
      with no "SalesInvoice" row behind it. Nothing to print, for anybody:
      not the rep, not the warehouse, not the order desk, not you.

      COUNTED AGAINST THE LIVE DATABASE. Six orders are in that state and three
      of them have already been delivered to customers:

          ORD-26-0140 Dispatched      ORD-26-0168 Dispatched
          ORD-26-0141 Dispatched      ORD-26-0170 Invoiced
          ORD-26-0143 To Order Dept   ORD-26-0171 Delivered

      The two routes now call the same method, so it cannot happen again. All
      six have been invoiced, on your say-so, through the real endpoint —
      see 7A for the numbers they were given and for the mistake I made doing
      it.

      It is idempotent. An order that already has an invoice does not get a
      second one; the step just makes sure the PDF exists. That is the other
      half of what you asked for, and it is enforced in the API rather than in
      the screen, so it holds however the endpoint is reached.


 1.2  "SALES RETURN SAYS Could not load invoices and refund methods"

      The Sales role did not hold invoices.view. Checked against the live
      database before changing anything — it held exactly five permissions:

          customers.manage, customers.view, orders.create, orders.view,
          reports.view, returns.sales

      returns.sales was there. invoices.view was not.

      The new-return screen loads the invoice list and the refund methods in
      one Promise.all. The invoice half answered 403, the whole call rejected,
      and the catch printed a sentence naming both halves and identifying
      neither. Sales returns were never broken. A rep was simply not allowed to
      look up the invoice they were returning against — and for the same
      reason could not open or print any bill, which is the other symptom you
      described.

      Two changes, and they are independent on purpose:

        · migration 18 grants invoices.view, invoices.create and returns.sales
          to the Sales role.
        · the screen now settles the two calls separately and says which one
          failed, in the API's own words. If this ever happens again you will
          be told what actually went wrong.

      PERMISSIONS ARE IN THE TOKEN. Everybody has to sign out and back in, or
      wait eight hours. See changa.txt §1.2.


 1.3  "NOBODY CAN SEE THE INVOICE AS A PDF"

      Two separate things, and only one of them was the one everybody assumed.

      The FIRST is 1.1 above: six orders had no invoice at all.

      The SECOND is a link that could be dead and nothing checking. Every bill
      is pushed to Cloudinary and the returned URL written to
      "SalesInvoice"."PdfUrl", and the screen opens that URL directly, because
      window.open cannot send an Authorization header. Cloudinary blocks PDF
      delivery by default on accounts created since 2023: the upload succeeds,
      an ordinary-looking secure_url comes back, and every request to it
      answers 401.

      PdfStore has always HEADed the URL after uploading and reported the
      answer. For a sale invoice that answer was used to choose the WhatsApp
      link and then thrown away — there was nowhere to keep it. So during the
      whole period the account was refusing PDFs, every Print button in the
      application opened Cloudinary's error page and the app had been told and
      had forgotten.

      CHECKED TODAY, AND IT IS SERVING. HEAD on a real stored bill answers 200.
      Somebody has ticked the box since convey.txt last said otherwise. Good —
      that is the better link. But the app now stores the answer per invoice
      instead of assuming either way, and re-checks a false one exactly once,
      so ticking or unticking that box never again silently breaks Print.

      TEN OF THE 38 INVOICES still have no stored PDF. Nothing is broken — a
      bill is rendered from the database whenever one is asked for — and the
      first time each is opened it archives itself. See changa.txt §2.2.

 2.  WHAT A SALESPERSON SEES NOW, EXACTLY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 HIS OWN: orders he raised, invoices against them, returns against those, and
 the customer accounts HE OPENED or has since been assigned.

 EVERYBODY'S: the customer PICKER on the order form. Deliberately — you said
 "issued still he can see all customers", and a rep covering for a colleague
 still has to be able to take the order. Administering an account and selling
 to one are different questions and they now have different answers.

 THE CREATOR IS A NEW FACT. "Party" carried only SalesPersonUserId, which is
 the rep the relationship is ASSIGNED to: often empty, and freely reassigned
 when a territory moves. Using it to answer "whose customers are these" would
 mean a rep loses their own account the day you hand it to somebody else. So
 there is now a separate "CreatedByUserId", written once and never edited.

 EXISTING ROWS were back-filled from the assigned rep, which is the closest
 true answer that exists — nothing recorded the creator before today. Parties
 with no rep are NULL and show only to the back office. That is correct: nobody
 can claim them. If some of them ought to belong to a particular rep, set the
 Sales Rep field on each and they will appear.

 The scoping is on the ROLE, not on a permission. Giving Sales the right to
 handle returns opens the returns screen to them; it does not turn them into
 the accounts department. Accounts, the order desk, the warehouse and you all
 see everything — you cannot reconcile a ledger through a keyhole.


───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 3.  THE SALES RETURN, END TO END
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 A rep raises it against one of their own invoices. The API refuses an invoice
 that is not theirs, refuses a product that is not on it, and refuses more
 units than were sold less whatever has already come back.

 IT GETS A DOCUMENT OF ITS OWN — a return note, not a second invoice. That is
 the distinction you drew and it is the right one: the order keeps the single
 bill it was billed on, because that number is printed on paper the customer is
 holding and a second bill for the same goods is how one sale gets paid for
 twice. What comes back is a different event with its own number (SR-…) and its
 own piece of paper.

 The note carries the invoice AND the order it came off, every line with its
 CONDITION, where each resalable unit went back to, and the credit due. The
 condition is on the face of it because it is the whole argument: a resalable
 unit is worth its price again, a damaged one is a loss somebody absorbs.

 It is archived to Cloudinary the moment the return is saved, in the same store
 and through the same renderer as every other document. Returns raised BEFORE
 today have no archived copy; their Print button renders from the database, so
 it works, and archives nothing.

 YOU ARE TOLD BY NAME. The notification goes to you, to Accounts and to the
 order desk (stock is coming back to a shelf they look after). Every copy now
 ends with who it was addressed to — see section 5.

 ON YOUR DASHBOARD it is a column of its own, in red, and a link. Count of
 returns, how many are waiting on a decision, units back, value credited, and
 the five most recent with the ORDER number rather than just the invoice —
 because the question you asked is which orders had goods come back. Clicking
 through goes to the list; clicking a row goes to the return, where the order,
 the bill, how many of the sold units came back, both PDFs and the whole
 activity trail are on one screen.

 4.  WHICH WAREHOUSE, WHICH ORDER DESK
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 NO NEW TABLE. The schema has modelled this since the first day and neither
 side was ever read:

     "User"."PrimaryLocationId"   the one place this person works at
     "UserLocation"               every place they may work out of

 What was missing was the rule. /admin/users would create a warehouse keeper
 with no location, or three, or attached to the Claim Stock shelf — and the
 picking queue then showed them every order in the company.

 Now: a keeper or an order-desk clerk gets EXACTLY ONE location, and it must be
 of the right kind — a warehouse for the keeper, a department for the desk. The
 form asks "which warehouse" and offers only warehouses; the API enforces the
 same rule, because a form is a convenience and not a guarantee.

 NOT "at least one". A keeper who belongs to two warehouses has an ambiguous
 queue, and the first thing anybody would ask on seeing it is which of the two
 the stock is actually on. If somebody genuinely covers both cities, give them
 two accounts or a back-office role — that is a decision about how the business
 is run, not a second tick-box.

 The warehouse queue is filtered to the keeper's own warehouse. You can still
 look at any warehouse's queue by passing a location. An account with no place
 set sees everything, which is what an admin looking at that page should see.

 THE SEED ONLY EVER HAD KARACHI, with two locations named "Warehouse" and
 "Order Department" — true when there was one city and confusing the moment
 there are two. Migration 18 renames them to "Karachi …" and adds the Lahore
 pair, so the dropdown has something to show. They are ordinary rows: rename
 them, deactivate them, add Faisalabad beside them. Nothing in the code knows
 any location's id or name. If you would rather set them up yourself, delete
 section 4 of migration 18 before running it.

 Every other role keeps the old behaviour — as many locations as you want to
 grant. An accountant reading ledgers is not standing anywhere in particular.

 5.  STOCK IN HAND, BY CITY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 /inventory/stock-levels now has a picker at the top: "Whole system — every
 city combined", then one entry per city with its units.

 THE CITY IS THE UNIT, not the shelf. Karachi has a warehouse and an order
 department; Lahore has its own pair. "How much do we hold in Lahore" means
 both of them added together, and a per-location view cannot answer it — half
 the stock is on the other shelf. The screen could previously only show every
 location side by side, which answers a different question and gets less
 readable with every city added.

 Inside a city the per-location columns stay, because it still matters whether
 something is on the warehouse shelf or already at the order desk. There are
 simply fewer of them, and only that city's.

 A row of city chips under the figures shows every city's units and value
 whatever the filter is set to — a breakdown that moves when you pick one of
 its own rows is a breakdown nobody can read, and comparing two towns is most
 of why anybody opens this screen.

 THE FILTER IS SERVER-SIDE. There are 957 items across five locations today and
 there will be more of both; pulling every balance and hiding most of them in
 the browser is the shape that stops working quietly.

 6.  NOTIFICATIONS: WHO IT IS FOR, AND A LINK THAT IS A LINK
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

 6.1  EVERY ONE NOW SAYS WHO IT WAS SENT TO

      "For Muhammad Zain (Warehouse Keeper)." on the end of the body, with the
      full name and the role in words.

      The same event goes to four or five people at once and the wording never
      said which copy you were holding. A keeper reading "Order invoiced" could
      not tell whether it had been addressed to them, to the order desk or to
      you. Read from the database, not from the token — the token belongs to
      whoever pressed the button, and this line is about whoever is reading it.

      The body is trimmed first if it would not otherwise fit. The column is
      300 characters and a row that overflows is a row that is thrown away
      silently, which is how long notifications used to disappear.

 6.2  THE LINK CARRIES THE REAL ADDRESS

      https://advpos-frontend.vercel.app/sales/orders/42, not /sales/orders/42.

      It comes from configuration — App:WebBaseUrl, overridable per environment
      with the App__WebBaseUrl variable — so you change one line and every
      notification, push and bell row follows. See changa.txt §1.3.

      Why it matters: a Web Push payload is opened by the service worker with
      clients.openWindow(), and a bare path resolves against the worker's scope
      rather than against your site. It happens to work while the app is the
      only thing on that origin. And a path pasted into WhatsApp is not a link.

      IN-APP NOTHING GOT SLOWER. The bell turns an absolute link back into a
      path when it points at the site you are already on, so clicking one is
      still a client-side navigation and not a full page load. A link pointing
      somewhere else — production's bell read on a local build — is left alone
      and opens as an ordinary link, which in that case is what it is.

      ROWS WRITTEN BEFORE TODAY still hold bare paths. They work exactly as
      they did.
